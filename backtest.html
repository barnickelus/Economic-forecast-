Methodology & honest caveats disclosure

Walk-forward: no look-ahead bias

On each historical day t, the engine computes signals using only data through day t-1, then "executes" the trade at day t's open price. Exits use day-t close prices. The same signal functions that drive the live dashboard run unchanged in the backtest.

Position sizing modes

Full Kelly: kelly f* (capped at ±1×) applied each day. Capital reinvests.
0.25× fractional Kelly: the conservative variant that matches our live dashboard. Most realistic.
Binary: 1× notional on consensus (≥3/4 agents), zero otherwise. Tests the consensus filter directly.

Transaction cost modeling

Default 10 bps round-trip = 5 bps in, 5 bps out. Realistic for silver futures at retail. For thinly-traded names, increase to 20-30 bps.

What this backtest can and can't tell you

Can: whether the signal stack would have made or lost money over the chosen window. Whether the consensus filter actually screens out losers. Whether Hurst regime weighting helps. Whether the probability forecasts are calibrated.

Can't: guarantee future performance. The window we're backtesting is limited; market regimes shift. Future returns may differ materially. This is a tool for evaluating model logic, not a profit projection.

Survivorship and selection bias

Yahoo data for currently-listed tickers only. For delisted stocks this matters; for futures (SI=F, GC=F) and ETFs (SPY, QQQ), survivorship is minimal. The default ticker (silver) is essentially unbiased on this dimension.
