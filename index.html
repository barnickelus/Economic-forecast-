Methodology — the negative-space framework disclosure

The thesis: institutional flow is invisible in raw price data but visible in its derivatives

Hedge funds, dealers, and dark-pool participants don't want their flow seen. They use VWAP algos, iceberg orders, and dark venues precisely because lit-market visibility would let competitors front-run them. But every hidden flow casts a shadow on observable public data — and that shadow has a characteristic shape. Renaissance and Citadel pay ~$50M/year for tick-level positioning data; we approximate the same information by computing derivatives and ratios of free public data that respond to that flow.

Signal 1: Gamma proxy via intraday vs close-to-close vol ratio

Dealers short gamma must delta-hedge by selling rallies and buying dips. This dampens intraday vol but doesn't dampen close-to-close vol, because hedging stops at the close. Compute intraday_range / |close_to_close|. When this ratio is high (≥3), dealers are short gamma and pinning the price. When it's low (≤1.5), dealers are long gamma and amplifying moves. The pin breaks abruptly when it does — usually on options expiry or major data.

Signal 2: Skew proxy via down-vol vs up-vol asymmetry

Put-call skew exists because everyone hedges in the same direction. Its shadow on price data: realized vol on down days is systematically larger than on up days. Compute downVol = sqrt(mean(r²) for r<0) − sqrt(mean(r²) for r>0) over a rolling window. When this gap widens, downside skew is rich — puts are being aggressively bid. When the gap compresses or inverts, the put hedge has been bought out — the next move down has no buyers because everyone is already hedged.

Signal 3: Dark-pool proxy via price-impact-per-volume (Kyle's lambda)

If a 2% price move happens on tiny lit volume, the actual flow happened off-tape (dark pools, OTC, block trades). Compute |return| / volume over a rolling window. When this ratio spikes, hidden flow is happening — usually institutional accumulation or distribution. The direction of the move tells you which side. This is the standard "price impact" coefficient from market microstructure (Kyle 1985).

Signal 4: Crowdedness velocity — the second derivative matters

You don't need to measure crowdedness levels — you need its rate of change. RSI moving from 50→80 in 3 sessions tells you more than RSI = 80 alone, because the acceleration is what reveals reflexive position-building. Compute the gradient (rate of change) and curvature (second derivative) of each crowdedness input. When inputs are accelerating together, positioning is building fast — the unwind will be violent.

Signal 5: Absorption via high-low/close-close ratio

If a day's high-low range is 4% but close-close is 0.5%, somebody fought hard and the market closed in the middle — that's absorption. Either huge buyers absorbed supply (bullish), or huge sellers absorbed demand (bearish). The next 1–3 sessions resolve which side won. This single signal has more predictive power for short-horizon turns than any other I've found, and it requires only OHLC.

Signal 6: Smart-money divergence via mining-stock spread

Silver miners (SIL, SILJ, HL, PAAS) reflect institutional theme allocation. They lead the metal by 3–10 days at turning points because allocators move into the theme before the commodity. When miners diverge from spot — especially when they underperform while spot makes new highs — institutional distribution is happening. Compute SIL_return - SI_return over rolling windows; persistent negative readings flag distribution.

Signal 7: Overnight-vs-intraday return decomposition

Algo and institutional flow concentrates during US/European session hours; retail flow concentrates overnight (Asian/electronic). Decomposing returns into intraday (RTH) and overnight (ETH) components reveals who is driving. Rallies happening overnight on light volume are retail FOMO (fragile). Rallies happening during NY hours on heavy volume are institutional (durable). When these diverge, the rally is suspect.

Why this matters — the math connecting to Kelly

Each negative-space signal contributes additional bits of information (per Kelly–Shannon) that wouldn't be available from price alone. The bits add nearly linearly when the signals are uncorrelated — gamma proxy and dark-pool proxy capture different flows, so their information is additive. The reflexivity multiplier (crowdedness) then modulates these bits: when crowd > 50%, all bullish bits are heavily discounted. This is the actual structural reason Medallion works — many uncorrelated low-edge signals, all sized by Kelly, all aware of their own crowdedness.
