Channel scope what this engine sees — and doesn’t

The dual-channel model was validated against the current Iran/Hormuz arc. Silver is moved by multiple geopolitical regimes simultaneously, and this engine only models one of them. The others can offset or amplify it independently. Calls here are one channel’s read, not a silver verdict.

✓ Modeled here: Iran / Hormuz / oil channel. De-escalation → oil falls → real rates ease → silver rises (relief). Escalation → oil rises → stagflation → silver falls. The headline tilt above is this channel’s read, anchored on the realized oil move and the deep deal-contract curve. Validated against this conflict’s news archive.

✗ NOT modeled here — active but invisible to the engine:

Real-rate / Fed channel. Silver pays no yield, so when real rates rise (Fed hawkish, dollar firm), silver falls independent of geopolitics. This is likely the biggest unmodeled driver. In 2022 the Fed hiking cycle dragged silver from $26 to $18 while Russia/Ukraine escalated — the macro channel completely overwhelmed any geopolitical bid. Recent confident-bullish reads losing against silver are most consistent with this channel quietly tightening.

Russia / Ukraine haven channel (smaller than I previously implied). Honest empirical check: silver got a ~6% bid in the 2 weeks AFTER the Feb 2022 invasion, then mean-reverted hard. Subsequent escalations (mobilization, annexations, Poland missile, first Oreshnik) produced little measurable silver move. So this channel exists but is short-lived shock-bid, not a multi-quarter regime — less load-bearing for silver than for gold.

Structural fiat / reserve channel. Slow multi-quarter: BRICS gold/silver accumulation, deficit trajectory, dollar reserve share. Probably the floor under silver’s $70s coil — structural buying as base while tactical channels noise the daily move.

Positioning / COT / gamma. Speculative positioning and dealer hedging extremes that can move silver against fundamentals. Unmeasured.

Implication: a bullish read on the Iran/oil channel does not equal a bullish silver outlook. If the Fed/real-rate channel is quietly firming (a dovish surprise priced out, or a strong dollar), silver can fall on falling oil because that macro channel loses more than the relief channel gains. The recent log pattern (confident bullish reads, silver down) is most consistent with that — the engine sees one channel and the tape is the sum of several, with the macro one likely dominant.

How it reasons — and its limits read me

The logic: (1) Each contract is split into level, Δ, days-to-expiry, depth, theme. (2) A near-dated contract falling toward zero is calendar decay, excluded from direction. (3) The regime is read from the deepest, longest-dated de-escalation contracts — their LEVEL says whether peace is structurally expected. (4) Escalation contracts only count when high-level AND rising. (5) Oil is the validated anchor and gets the dominant vote; when contracts disagree with oil, confidence collapses to a no-call.

The limits: it cannot tell you if the signal leads or lags price — only the log can. It's a transparent rule set, not a crystal ball; it can be wrong when the rules don't fit the moment. For a deeper, free-form geopolitical read on any given day, paste the data table into a conversation — that's the AI reasoning layer, which can't run on a public page but runs fine in dialogue.
